Design notes & cross-check

CHECK Common-anode RGB needs inverted drive

The LED's anode sits on +3V3, so each cathode lights when its GPIO is pulled LOW. The sketch's setLED() writes values straight through (e.g. setLED(255,0,0) for "waiting"), which would drive red off and the other two on. The code even flags this with // Invert values if using common anode LED — apply the inversion (write 255 - v) for the intended colours.

MATCH Motor & LED pins verified

AIN1=22, AIN2=23, BIN1=18, BIN2=19 and LED_R=16, LED_G=17, LED_B=4 in the sketch match this schematic exactly, including the PWM channel assignments (CH_A1…CH_B_, CH_R/G/B).

Driver always enabled

DRV8833 STBY is tied directly to +3V3, so the H-bridge is permanently active — there is no sleep pin under firmware control. Direction and speed are set purely by the four PWM lines.

Decoupling capacitors

C1 10 µF electrolytic + C2 0.1 µF ceramic on VIN (BAT+ rail); C3 10–22 µF bulk + C4 0.1 µF ceramic on VOUT (+3V3). C5 0.1 µF debounces the EN reset line. Keep them close to the regulator pins.

Flashing & UART

The 1×5 header breaks out 3.3V, TX (GPIO1), RX (GPIO3), GPIO0 and GND. Connect the programmer's RX to the ESP32 TX and vice-versa; hold GPIO0 LOW (button/jumper to GND) while resetting to enter the bootloader.

Power tree & thermal note

Battery + flows to DRV8833 VM (motor rail) and AMS1117 VIN. The LDO output is the single +3V3 rail feeding the ESP32, driver logic, RGB anode and header. The AMS1117 dissipates the (8.4 V − 3.3 V) drop, so size its thermal pad for the ESP32 + logic load.

Reset circuit

EN has a 10 kΩ pull-up to +3V3, a tactile button to GND, and a 0.1 µF debounce cap — the standard ESP32 auto-reset front end.
